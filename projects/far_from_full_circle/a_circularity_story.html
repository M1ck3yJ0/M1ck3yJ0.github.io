Far from Full Circle

In 2022, just 11.4% of the material the EU used came back into use from waste. By 2030, the EU wants that share to reach 24%, more than double. Over the past twenty years, it has risen by about a fifth of a point a year. Getting there now would take about 1.6 points a year. This is a story about what that goal truly asks of us.

Picture the target as a ring. The gold arc is what the EU recycles, and the thin blue circle is 24% of everything it uses. When the arc reaches all the way round, the target is met. In 2022, it reached less than halfway. In the middle is the material the EU consumed that year: 6.4 billion tonnes, about 14 tonnes for every person, or 39 kilos a day. There are two ways to close the ring. We can recycle more, so the arc grows longer, or we can use less, so the circle grows smaller.

Recycling can only work with what comes back as waste, and much of what we use never does. Fuel is burned and gone, its carbon released into the air. Food is eaten. Stone, sand and steel become homes and roads that stand for decades. Each year, the waste that does come back weighs as much as 31% of the material the EU consumes. And recycling it doesn't only lengthen the arc. Recycled material counts as material used too, so every tonne recycled also widens the circle a little.

So imagine the EU recycled every tonne that comes back, the rubble, soil, stone and ash included. The arc would reach 99% of the way round, a rate of 23.7%, and still fall just short. Recycling all of it isn't practical, either. Even perfect recycling, on its own, can't close the ring.

Now the other lever. Keep recycling where it was in 2022, and use less instead. As the material in the middle shrinks, the circle tightens around the arc, until the arc's two ends finally meet. That happens when the EU uses 59.3% less material than it did in 2022: about 8.5 of those 14 tonnes per person. It's a great deal to ask. But it's the only lever that can close the ring on its own.

So the EU's target, which reads like a goal for recycling, is in effect a goal for using less. It isn't a choice between the two; we need both. But there's no realistic route to 24% that doesn't run through using much less. Even if the EU recycled 70% of its waste instead of 41%, it would still need to use about 31% less material.

The European Environment Agency came to the same conclusion. It modeled the EU recycling 70% of its waste, using 15% less material and a third less fossil fuel, all at once. Even then, the rate reached only about 22% by 2030.
